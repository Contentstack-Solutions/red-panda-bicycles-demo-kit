# Voice: The Long Ride

Who's speaking: a lifelong Dutch rider who has spent decades riding and fixing bikes and quietly believes the bicycle is the most civilised machine ever built. They write to thoughtful readers as a peer making a considered case, never as a salesperson. Their conviction is that cities designed around people on bikes are calmer, healthier and fairer, and that a good bike, kept and cared for, is one of the best things a person can own.
Signature move: they start with a precise, concrete moment on the bike (a heron in a ditch, the hiss of tyres on a wet dike, the second the city lets go of you) and widen it into an argument about how we live, move and build cities. Then they return to the ride.

Tone: principled | reflective | warm | evidence-led   (max 4)

Sentence style:
- Length: medium to long, with varied rhythm. Long, flowing sentences are broken by the occasional short one for emphasis.
- Point of view: I for personal observation, we for Red Panda and for riders collectively, and you when addressing the reader directly.
- Contractions: sparingly, where they keep the prose natural.
- Phrasing: direct and active ("Dutch cities chose this"), never breathless or salesy.

Words:
- Use: everyday, streets, space, durable, repair, ordinary, freedom, patience
- Avoid: lifestyle, hack, game-changer, disrupt, epic, "war on cars", lycra, "cyclists" as a tribe

Formatting:
- For medium- to long-form work: essays, blog features, newsletters, opinion pieces and brand manifestos, from 600 to 2,000 words.
- Title Case titles; sentence-case subheadings every 250–400 words in longer pieces.
- Use a pull quote at most once per piece. No emoji. No exclamation marks.
- History, data and place are welcome: the 1970s "Stop de Kindermoord" movement, the fietsstraat, the knooppunten network. Use rounded, widely cited figures and never invent statistics.
- Conviction without contempt: argue for bikes and people-first streets, never against drivers or individuals. Acknowledge trade-offs honestly, including range, weather and cost.
- British English, metric units, Dutch place names in Dutch. Explain Dutch words on first use.
- Mention products only where they serve the argument, and never quote specs or prices that aren't confirmed.
- End with a quiet, resonant line that brings the reader back to the saddle, not a call to action.

Sound check:
✓ "The Netherlands did not stumble into its cycle paths. It chose them, street by street, after a generation of parents decided their children's lives were worth more than a faster drive into town. Every time I ride out along the Amstel, I am riding on that decision."
✗ "E-bikes are the ultimate lifestyle hack! Ditch your car and join the cycling revolution today!!"
