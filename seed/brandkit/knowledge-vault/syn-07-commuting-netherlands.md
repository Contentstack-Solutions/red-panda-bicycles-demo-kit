# Commuting by bike in and around Amsterdam

Practical background for commuter-focused content.

## Who commutes into Amsterdam by bike
Many riders commute from inside the city: Oost, West, Noord, Zuid and Nieuw-West. Plenty more come from surrounding towns such as Amstelveen, Zaandam, Haarlem, Almere, Purmerend and Hoofddorp, where 10–25 km each way is common. For those distances an e-bike turns a sweaty workout into an ordinary commute, and the headwind stops being the deciding factor.

## Choosing the right bike for the distance
- Under about 5 km: a City Bike such as The Jordaan or the Metro Hybrid is quick, simple and easy to park.
- 5–25 km: an E-Bike. Our commuter e-bikes (RP Roam, and the Glide E-Commuter for lighter riding) are designed for daily distance in all weathers.
- Children, shopping or a dog on board: the Cargo Cub E-Cargo Bike.

## E-bike rules in brief
- Standard e-bikes assist up to 25 km/h. No licence, number plate or helmet requirement, and they use normal cycle paths.
- Speed pedelecs assist up to 45 km/h and are legally mopeds: yellow plate, moped licence (AM), insurance and an approved helmet. Inside built-up areas they generally have to use the road, not the cycle path.

## Money matters (keep it general)
- Many Dutch employers pay a tax-free travel allowance per kilometre for commuting by bike. Rates change, so don't quote a number.
- Under the "fiets van de zaak" scheme, employers can lease a bike to employees, who pay a small taxable addition (7% of the bike's value per year) instead of buying it outright.

## Amsterdam commuting tips
- The free GVB ferries behind Amsterdam Centraal are part of the road network for anyone commuting from Noord.
- Park at a guarded or station bike garage when you can. At many stations the first 24 hours are free.
- Avoid the Damrak and the busiest tourist streets at rush hour. Parallel routes are faster and calmer.
- Use two locks: the frame lock plus a chain or U-lock to something fixed.
- Keep rain gear in your pannier all year. The forecast is a suggestion, not a promise.

## Commuter kit checklist
Commuter Pannier 20L (laptop, lunch, rain gear), front and rear lights (Beacon 1200 and Tail Glow), Fortress U-Lock, rain jacket and trousers, overshoes, and a charged battery. Charge at work or at home; don't leave it to the morning.

## What commuters tell us they care about
Reliability comes first: the bike has to work every single morning. Next is real-world range, meaning enough for a return trip into a headwind in January without range anxiety. After that come comfort, weather protection, easy servicing and a lock setup that means the bike is still there at 18:00.
