# Red Panda editorial style guide

Rules for all Red Panda written content: web, blog, email, social and product copy.

## Spelling and units
- British English: favourite, neighbourhood, colour, tyre, kerb, metre, organise.
- Metric only: km, km/h, kg, °C. Write "25 km", "25 km/h".
- Euros with the symbol before the number and no space: €89. Use prices only if they come from the live product page.
- Dates: 12 September 2026. Times: 24-hour clock, 10:00.

## Names
- "Red Panda Bicycles" on first mention, then "Red Panda". Never "RPB".
- Product names exactly as in the catalogue: RP Roam, RP Volt, The Jordaan (capital T), Cargo Cub E-Cargo Bike.
- Categories as on the website: City Bikes, E-Bikes, Lifestyle Bikes, Accessories. "E-Bike" is capitalised and hyphenated.
- Amsterdam BC is our riders' club; write "Amsterdam BC members".
- Dutch place names in Dutch: Amsterdam Centraal, Amsterdamse Bos, Ouderkerk aan de Amstel, Den Haag (use "The Hague" only for international audiences). Keep the capital IJ: IJ, IJmeer, IJsselmeer.
- Dutch words are welcome in small doses. Explain them the first time: fietspad (cycle path).

## Headlines
- Article titles in Title Case: "Best Biking Day Trips Around Amsterdam".
- Subheadings in sentence case: "Six rides worth a whole day".
- Numbered listicles are fine when the list is genuinely useful.

## Riding culture and safety
- Show riding as normal, everyday and done in normal clothes.
- Never show or describe riding with a phone in hand (illegal in the Netherlands since 2019), riding without lights after dark, or riding against traffic.
- Helmets: most Dutch adults ride city bikes without one. We neither shame nor preach. Speed pedelecs legally require an approved helmet, and we say so.
- Always two locks: the frame lock plus a chain or U-lock to something fixed.
- Respect everyone on the road. No jokes at the expense of drivers, pedestrians, tourists or other brands. Gentle self-deprecation about Amsterdam riders is fine.

## Claims
- No absolute range claims. Real-world range depends on wind, assist level, load, temperature and tyre pressure. Say so.
- No invented statistics, specs, awards or prices.
- Distances in articles: say whether a figure is straight-line ("as the crow flies") or a riding route.

## Recurring motifs that feel like us
Headwinds with opinions, apple cake the size of a paving stone, herons that refuse to move, the free ferries behind Centraal, "overshoes first", and the moment the city lets go of you on the way out of town.

## Words we avoid
Game-changer, revolutionary, unleash, epic, crush it, hack, "cyclist" as a tribe (prefer "people on bikes" or "riders"), and anything that makes cycling sound like an extreme sport.
