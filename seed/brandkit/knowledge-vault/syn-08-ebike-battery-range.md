# E-bike batteries and real-world range: what riders should know

General guidance on lithium-ion e-bike batteries, written the way Red Panda talks about them: honestly. Model-specific specs, warranty terms and range figures belong on the product and warranty pages. Never invent them.

## Why "range" is always a range
The range printed in a catalogue is measured under favourable test conditions. On the road, range depends on:
- **Wind**: in the Netherlands, the single biggest factor. A stiff headwind can cut range dramatically.
- **Assist level**: turbo mode empties a battery far faster than eco.
- **Temperature**: lithium-ion batteries deliver noticeably less in the cold, so winter range is shorter.
- **Load**: rider, luggage, children, shopping.
- **Tyre pressure**: soft tyres cost range. Pump them up every couple of weeks.
- **Stop-start riding**: accelerating away from every traffic light costs more than steady cruising.
The honest advice: learn your own range on your own commute, in both summer and winter, and plan with a margin.

## Battery care that actually matters
- **Charge indoors at room temperature.** Don't charge a battery that is below freezing; let it warm up first.
- **Store it partly charged.** For weeks off the bike (a winter break, say), leave the battery at roughly half charge in a cool, dry place indoors, and top it up every month or two.
- **Don't leave it on the charger for days.** Unplug once it's full.
- **Use the original charger** and check cables and connectors for damage.
- **Keep it clean, not blasted.** E-bikes are made for rain, but never use a pressure washer on the battery or motor.
- **Remove the battery** when carrying the bike on a car rack.

## Battery myths
- *"You must run it flat before charging."* False. That was true of old nickel batteries, not lithium-ion. Top up whenever you like.
- *"Charging to 100% every time ruins it."* Not really. Charging full before a long ride is exactly what the battery is for. Storing it at 100% for months is what to avoid.
- *"Rain will kill it."* No. E-bikes are designed for Dutch weather. Submersion and pressure washers are the real enemies.
- *"The box says 100 km, so I'll get 100 km."* Only with the wind behind you, on eco mode, on a mild day.
- *"Batteries die suddenly after a year or two."* Batteries lose capacity gradually over years of use. A slow drop in range is normal ageing, not failure. A sudden drop is worth a workshop check.
