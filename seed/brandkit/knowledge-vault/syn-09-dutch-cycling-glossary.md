# Dutch cycling glossary

Dutch words that come up when riding in the Netherlands. Use them sparingly in content, and explain them on first use.

- **fiets**: bicycle. *Op de fiets* means by bike.
- **fietser**: a person on a bike.
- **fietspad**: a separated cycle path, often in red asphalt.
- **fietsstraat**: a bicycle street where cars are guests ("auto te gast").
- **fietsenstalling**: bike parking, from a rack to a vast station garage.
- **fietsenrek**: a bike rack.
- **fietsenmaker**: a bike mechanic or repair shop. Every neighbourhood has one.
- **omafiets**: "granny bike", the classic upright black Dutch bike. **Opafiets** is the men's version.
- **bakfiets**: a cargo bike with a box at the front, usually full of children.
- **fietsbel**: a bicycle bell, legally required and socially essential.
- **lekke band**: a flat tyre.
- **tegenwind**: headwind. The national sport of complaining about it is optional but popular.
- **meewind**: tailwind. Rare, fleeting, treasured.
- **uitwaaien**: literally "to blow out". Going outside in the wind to clear your head. Best done on a bike along a dike.
- **knooppunt**: a numbered junction in the national cycling route network.
- **snelfietsroute**: a cycle highway between towns, built for commuters.
- **haaientanden**: "shark's teeth", the white road triangles that mean give way.
- **OV-fiets**: rental bikes at train stations.
- **gladheid**: slippery conditions, usually ice.
- **gezellig**: cosy, convivial, pleasant. A coffee stop with friends on a ride is very gezellig.
- **appeltaart met slagroom**: apple cake with whipped cream. The official fuel of Dutch day trips.
- **Hup!**: a cheer of encouragement, shouted at people climbing a (small) hill.
