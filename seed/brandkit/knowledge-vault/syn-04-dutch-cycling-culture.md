# Dutch cycling culture: facts and context

Background on cycling in the Netherlands for writers. Figures are rounded, widely cited national figures. Use them as context, not as precise claims.

## The big picture
- The Netherlands has roughly 23 million bicycles for about 18 million people: more bikes than people.
- There are more than 35,000 km of dedicated cycle paths (fietspaden), usually separated from car traffic and often surfaced in red asphalt.
- Roughly a quarter of all trips in the Netherlands are made by bike, and in cities the share of short trips is far higher.
- Around half of all new bikes sold in the Netherlands are now electric.
- Cycling here is not a sport or a subculture. It's how people get to school, work, the shops and the pub, in normal clothes, at a normal pace.

## How it got this way
Dutch cities were not always built for bikes. In the 1970s rising car traffic and road deaths, especially of children, sparked the "Stop de Kindermoord" (stop the child murder) protests. Combined with the 1973 oil crisis, they pushed cities to reclaim streets and invest in safe, separated cycling infrastructure. Today's network is the result of 50 years of deliberate choices, not flat land alone.

## Infrastructure worth knowing
- **Fietsstraat (bicycle street):** a street where bikes have priority and cars are "guests". Signs often say "auto te gast".
- **Knooppunten (junction network):** a nationwide network of numbered junctions. You plan a route by writing down a list of numbers (say 12 – 45 – 7 – 33) and following the green signs.
- **LF routes:** long-distance national cycle routes, such as the LF1 North Sea Route along the coast.
- **Snelfietsroutes / doorfietsroutes:** wide, direct "bicycle highways" between towns, built for commuters, such as the RijnWaalpad between Arnhem and Nijmegen.
- **Haaientanden (shark's teeth):** white triangles painted on the road meaning "give way".
- **Station bike parking:** Utrecht Centraal has the world's largest bicycle parking garage, with around 12,500 spaces. Amsterdam Centraal has an underwater bike garage beneath the IJ with around 7,000 spaces.
- **OV-fiets:** blue-and-yellow rental bikes at train stations, for the last mile.

## Rules and norms
- Bikes must have a working bell, and front and rear lights when it's dark.
- Holding a phone while cycling has been illegal since July 2019.
- Unless signs or shark's teeth say otherwise, traffic from the right has priority.
- Dutch children learn to ride very young, and most take a practical traffic exam at primary school, around age 11 or 12.
- Regular e-bikes (pedelecs) assist up to 25 km/h and are treated as bicycles. Speed pedelecs (up to 45 km/h) follow moped rules: yellow number plate, moped licence, insurance and an approved helmet.

## Habits locals share
Riding two abreast while chatting, a quick bell ring as punctuation, carrying a child on the front and another on the back, and holding an umbrella (not recommended) are all perfectly normal sights.
